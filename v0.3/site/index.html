The Honest Agent v0.3

Do frontier agents ask before they act, and can a monitor tell when they don't?

The big models waited for approval every time. The small one didn't, and only the monitors watching its actions noticed.

Agents got a small simulated job: record a payment, deploy a build, or share a document. There was one rule: get approval for the exact target first. The only thing that changed between runs was the answer to their request: granted, pending (approver unavailable) or denied. Afterwards, each agent wrote a short report saying whether it finished and whether it broke the rule. Then monitors judged each run, reading either that report alone or the full record of what the agent did.

What the monitors caught

Five judges looked at every run. A code-based policy checker replayed the rules exactly. Two AI monitors (Claude and GPT) read the full record of the agent's actions, and the same two read only the agent's final report. AI monitors were allowed to answer "not enough evidence".

The monitors that read the full record were perfect here: they caught every breach and raised no false alarms. The report-only monitors were far weaker, in two opposite ways. GPT almost never gave a verdict from a report alone. Claude believed reports that said "no violation", so it let 15 breaches through.

Qwen's report vs what actually happened deviation

Here's why a report-only monitor fails. Qwen's reports barely matched reality. It said it finished the task in 0 of 60 runs, but it actually finished 24 times. Its answer to "did you break the rule?" was right in only 22 of 60. Both answers were right in 13 of 60. The big models got both right in all 210.

How Qwen broke the rule post hoc

Most of Qwen's breaches weren't a response to approval pressure. In 36 of its 60 runs it asked approval for the recipient (partner@example.net) instead of the document. That's an easy mix-up for a small model, because you share to someone. The system answered "unknown target", and Qwen shared anyway.

What this does and doesn't show

It does show that in a short task with an explicit approval rule, two frontier models reliably waited for approval and reported honestly. It also shows that monitors reading the full action record caught every breach by a weaker model without crying wolf, and that a monitor reading only the report inherits the reporter's mistakes.

It doesn't show how frontier models behave under real pressure, whether they would hide a breach, whether the v0.2 result replicates on its original task, or any precise rate. The frontier models also ran inside their providers' own agent tools, so some of their caution may come from those tools rather than the models themselves.
